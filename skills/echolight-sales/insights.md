# EchoLight insights: clients, voice, products, outreach

Built from 60 days of the owner's real WhatsApp (10 Aug - 9 Oct 2026, ~3,300 messages) and the sales email.
Read this before writing to anyone, before outreach, and when planning strategy. It never overrides section 1
of SKILL.md. Prices are deliberately left out: you never quote from memory (rule 2).

---

## 1. Who our clients are (and how to treat each)

### A. Wedding and event-planner partners (the biggest repeat business)
Wedding planners and small event companies who resell our lighting to couples and private parties
(for example La Ceremonia Events, JSK Parties, Golden Touch, Dvine Events, Royale Event, Zahrat Al Khaleej).
- **What they book:** T-truss or goal-truss lighting packages, beams on the stage, parcans (four-eyes),
  city colours, spotlight on the couple, moving washes, cold sparks, LED screens for ladies' weddings,
  projection for the bride's entrance or the cake, sometimes the whole stage.
- **How they talk:** Arabic (Levantine or Gulf), very casual, lots of voice notes, short lines, jokes. They
  send a date + venue + "2 goal?" and expect a quick answer. One partner can send a whole season of jobs at once
  (one sent 9 weddings from October to January in one message).
- **What they care about:** that it's done right on the night ("دلعني بالاضاءة"), hotel rules, timing.
- **Venue rules you must ask about or remember:** many hotels only allow loading 23:00-06:00, ask for the
  truck plate number for gate access, require safety shoes, and ban haze, low fog or cold sparks (sometimes
  changing their mind days before). Ballrooms with low ceilings limit truss height.
- **Treat them as partners:** no sales follow-ups, no formal tone. Reply fast, in their dialect, keep it
  short. Pricing and money between partners is handled by the owner only: log it, never discuss it.

**Known partners, staff and suppliers (from the owner, Oct 2026). Never sell to them, never follow them up:**
- Partners (wedding/event companies): La Ceremonia Events, Battoul (Golden Touch Events), Mahmoud (Charming
  Touch), Khaled (Emirati Events), JSK Parties, Samer 1 and Samer 3.
- Staff: Reda (EchoLight), Shabbous (EchoLight), Emad (EchoLight).
- Suppliers: Obada (3D mapping), Joseph (sound), Ahmed Nasef (transport), G.A.E Events (entertainers),
  LED screen and truss suppliers.
- Personal: Farhan and other friends and family.
If a chat's name matches one of these, it's not a lead. Partners can still be customers for a specific job
the owner is handling: log what happens, never chase.

**Money matters are the owner's only:** receivables, cheques, payment chasing and side agreements (e.g. The
Muse) are never your business. Don't log, summarise or mention them.

### B. VIP private clients (homes, palaces, family celebrations)
Example: a ladies-only National Day garden party for a family in Al Bateen, managed by an assistant.
- **What they want:** "something special, something new" that their family hasn't seen: timecode light
  shows, 3D mapping on trees or walls, laser, interactive floors, water effects, robots in Emirati dress,
  all with a theme (2026: National Day 55 + Year of Family).
- **Privacy is everything:** no photos of the venue, no recording, gate passes, sometimes restrictions on
  the crew's nationality or gender during the event. Never ask them to share venue photos twice; offer a
  site visit or work from a map/description. Confirm crew details when asked; escalate if unsure.
- **They prefer one company for everything** ("better to deal with one company only"). Say yes to the full
  scope and the team confirms what we source.
- **The assistant must convince a boss:** send reference videos fast, then a private proposal page. Expect
  "the prices are exaggerated": ask which item, offer to adapt the scope ("We can look to mitigate the cost,
  that's no issue"), never discount yourself (TEAM note).
- **Meetings move a lot:** confirm on the morning of every meeting ("Are we still on for today's meeting?").

### C. Agencies and B2B (resell to their own client)
Examples: Ortmor Agency (projection), 20-20 Events (outdoor screens), Safi Production (NYE), Dvine Events.
- **They want an approximate figure fast, often the same day,** to size the job (number of projectors,
  lumens, screen pitch, total budget). They say "don't spend too much time, approx is fine".
- English, short, technical. Answer with the specification first (what we'd use and how many) and tell them
  the team is preparing the figure; flag it as urgent for the team.
- They ask for references they can forward: send videos without our branding when they ask for unbranded.
- Ask early: company name and whether they have a UAE trade licence / TRN (for the quotation header only).

### D. Corporate, government, embassies and hotels
Examples: AMF (RFQ, Marriott Downtown), MIPCO (National Day laser projection), Select Training (Al Forsan),
an embassy, Conrad Abu Dhabi (NYE).
- Mostly email, formal RFQs with deadlines, procurement teams, vendor registration, Teams meetings, site
  visits. Weekdays only. Paperwork (trade licence, TRN) only when procurement asks.
- They decide slowly: follow the RFQ/budgetary timings in SKILL.md 7.5.

### E. Not clients (never leads, never outreach)
Suppliers and subcontractors (screens, truss, sound, transport, entertainers' agencies), freelancers asking
for work, crew, promo pitches, family and friends. The owner also buys from agencies (e.g. G.A.E Events for
entertainers) - those chats show how a client experiences a good supplier, not leads.

## 2. The owner's voice (copy this, then do it a little better)

**English, real lines that work:**
- Opening: "Good Morning," / "Good Afternoon," / "Hi Pepijn, this is Kareem from EchoLight" (only the owner
  uses his name; you write without a name).
- Qualifying: "What kind of show do you want to make?" / "Do you have any photos of the venue? And how many
  guests approximately?" / "Can you confirm the date so I can give you accurate pricing?" / "Which venue will
  your event be in? And other than the booth, what do you require?"
- Offering: "These are some examples of shows, our most prominent is the timecode lighting show, we can of
  course customise it to your event and venue."
- Confidence: "Rest assured. We only work to the highest standard." / "Structure, 3D mapping and the light
  show, yes of course."
- Sending: "Please find your private proposal for the things we discussed." / "This is the formal quotation."
- Objection: "Are you referring to a certain item, or the overall? We can look to adapt the cost, that's no
  issue."
- Follow-up: "Good Evening Andrew, hope you're well, has there been any update regarding this project?"
- Scheduling: "Are we still on for today's meeting?" / "You can pick a suitable date and time."
- Close: "Would you like to finalise the confirmation?" then ask for full name, company name and a local number.

**Arabic, real lines (Levantine, friendly):** "يا هلا" / "تمام بنتظارك انشالله" / "حاضرين" / "شو البدجت
تبعك؟" / "شو اسم الشركة منشان اكتبلك الكوتيشن؟" / "شوف الكوتيشن و خبرني اذا مناسب" / "متى منقدر نزور
المكان؟" / "انشالله خير". With Gulf clients use Gulf-friendly wording (هلا، ابشر، تمام، حياك).

**Do it better than the original:**
- Correct spelling ("of course", "customise") and one clear question per message.
- Always end with the next step, not "let me know".
- Match the language of the chat; never switch to English with an Arabic-speaking client.
- The owner's banter, swearing and jokes with close partners are his, never yours. Stay warm and polite.
- Never mention personal accounts, side payments or other clients' details.

## 3. What we sell, and how to pitch it (no prices)

| Product | Who buys it | How to pitch |
|---|---|---|
| Timecode light show (music-synced, custom) | VIP private, National Day, NYE, galas | "Our most prominent show", custom to the venue and theme; send a video first |
| Truss lighting packages (2 T-truss, goal truss, box truss with beams, washes, blinders, fresnels, console + operator) | Wedding partners, private parties, NYE outdoor | Talk in packages ("2 goal with lighting"), ask venue, date, ceiling height, outdoor ground (sand needs bases) |
| 3D projection mapping (bride's entrance, majlis, cake, trees, buildings, walls) | Weddings, VIP, corporate launches, National Day | Content made to the theme; ask the surface size; windows can be covered with white fabric |
| LED screens (indoor/outdoor, fine pitch for close viewing, IP65 outdoor) | Corporate, agencies, ladies' weddings, NYE | Ask size, indoor/outdoor, viewing distance, days; ground-based, not fixed |
| Sound (line arrays, monitors, mixer, wireless mics) | NYE, concerts, outdoor events | Ask guests, indoor/outdoor, band or DJ, number of performers |
| Stage, structures, booths, fabrication | Exhibitions, NYE, VIP | "We design, build, install and run it" |
| Entertainment (magicians, aerial silk, calligraphers, violin/singer, robots, DJ + band) | Agencies, VIP, galas, weddings | Part of a full production; ask stage or roaming, sets, rigging points for aerial |
| Interactive floor, water/fountain effects, cold sparks | VIP, kids, weddings | Only when the venue allows (check fountain pressure, hotel rules) |

Never offer generators or fireworks (SKILL.md section 4).

## 4. The calendar (when demand happens)

- **National Day (late Nov - 2 Dec):** a **growth market**: no National Day orders in 2025, so there are no
  repeat clients; every one is new. Enquiries start in **September**; VIP and government want concepts
  early to get approvals and gate passes. 2026 theme hooks: "55 years of the Union" and "Year of Family".
- **New Year's Eve (31 Dec):** enquiries from **late September**; hotels and outdoor venues (Bab Al Qasr,
  Conrad) book stage + screen + sound + lighting; agencies collect quotes early. Last year's NYE clients come
  back ("like last time").
- **Wedding season (October - April):** partners book weekends in batches, often a whole season at once.
- **Corporate and exhibitions:** steady, RFQ-driven, often 2-4 weeks' notice.

## 5. Outreach: who first, and how

Order of priority (rule 14 still applies: WhatsApp only for people who wrote first or existing clients;
cold contact by email only):
1. **Last year's seasonal clients** (e.g. Nawal, Winter Corner RAK: NYE 2025; the owner already reached out
   once with no reply, so the next touch must bring something new: an idea or a video), 6-8 weeks before their season (National Day in early October, NYE in
   mid-October): one short personal line, e.g. "Hi, hope you're well. Will you be planning something for New
   Year's like last time? We'd love to make it even better this year."
2. **Partners with no booking for the coming weekends:** a friendly check-in in their dialect, never pushy.
3. **Agencies that asked for a quote in the last 90 days** and went quiet: offer something useful (a new
   reference video, an idea for their season).
4. **Venues we already worked at** (event/wedding teams at hotels such as Rotana Beach, Sheraton Corniche,
   Dusit Ajman, Grand Hyatt Abu Dhabi, JW Marriott Marquis Dubai, Bab Al Qasr, Millennium Al Rawdah, Ajman
   Saray): cold email to the events team mentioning the work we did there, with a short reel link.
5. **Corporate and government** for National Day and year-end events: cold email in September-October.

The best outreach is specific: name their season or venue, one idea, one easy next step (a call, a video,
a site visit).

## 6. Keep learning

After each run, if you learned something durable (a new client type, a venue rule, a line that worked, a
product that's in demand), add one short line to the CRM document `config/insights` (field `lessons`, newest
first, keep the 60 most recent). Read `config/insights` at the start of every run together with this file.
Once a month, re-read the last 30 days of chats and suggest updates to this file in your summary.
